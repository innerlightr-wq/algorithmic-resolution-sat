# Addendum, 2026-10-27 — coverage-matching wording, and what the extension adds

This addendum corrects a **wording** problem in the project's own summaries. It
changes **no number and no result**. The frozen Phase 1–4 reports under
`docs/validation/` are left exactly as written; this file records the correction
and the extension that prompted it.

---

## 1. The correction: "matched coverage" was applied to the wrong denominator

Two different quantities were being described with the same phrase.

| | n | paired Wilcoxon p | mean `ε̂_W` | mean `ε̂_C` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **all paired held-out instances** | **349** | **5.1 × 10⁻³⁸** | 1.413 | 3.516 |
| **approximately coverage-matched subset** (coverage within 10 percentage points) | **160** | **2.5 × 10⁻⁸** | 0.806 | 1.625 |

Both are read directly from the frozen record, `docs/validation/phase4/PAIRED_OBSERVER_TEST.md`,
which states them correctly and keeps them apart.

**What was wrong.** The root `README.md` described the 349-instance result as
holding "at matched or near-matched coverage", and `docs/SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md`
labels rows B5 and B6 "Matched-coverage paired … (349 instances)". Both readings
attach *coverage matching* to the full 349. They should not.

**The correct statement.**

* The `p = 5.1 × 10⁻³⁸` resolution result belongs to **all 349 paired
  instances**, at each solver's own standard budget — **not** to a
  coverage-matched set.
* Coverage matching is a **restriction** to the **160** instances where the two
  solvers' coverage levels fall within 10 percentage points, and there the
  resolution result is `p = 2.5 × 10⁻⁸`.
* The same distinction applies to the fragmentation result: `p = 5.2 × 10⁻³¹`
  on all 349, surviving the coverage-matched restriction at `p = 2.4 × 10⁻⁷`
  on the same 160.

The root `README.md` has been corrected. `docs/SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md` and the
Phase 1–4 validation reports are **frozen historical records and have not been
edited**; read their B5/B6 row labels subject to this addendum.

This is a labelling fix, not a retraction. The paper's substantive claim — that
the resolution ordering survives restricting to coverage-matched instances —
is unchanged and was always supported by the n = 160 figure.

## 2. Why it surfaced

The 2026-10-27 extension (`extensions/certified-resolution-2026-10-27/`) tested
the ordering under a *different* control: instead of restricting to instances
where coverage happened to match, it **equalises the number of distinct
recorded solutions** by uniform subsampling. Setting that up required stating
precisely what the September work had and had not controlled for, which is
where the conflation became visible.

## 3. Distinctions to use when summarising either body of work

These are easy to blur and each has bitten this project at least once:

1. **Equal cardinality on the same formula implies exactly equal coverage
   fractions**, since `|A|/|S_I|` shares one constant denominator. The
   numerical fraction can still be **entirely unknown** — and is, because
   `|S_I|` is not computed beyond `n ≤ 20`. Equal-coverage and
   known-coverage are different statements.
2. **Random subsampling is uniform within a recorded set, not within `S_I`.**
   It makes a set uniform with respect to itself; it cannot repair a recorded
   set that was unevenly spread to begin with.
3. **A higher lower bound alone does not prove one true resolution exceeds
   another.** With interval-valued measurements, an ordering is established
   only when the intervals are disjoint. Overlapping intervals establish
   nothing — in particular not equality.
4. **The seven-instance equal-size study does not establish a universal solver
   ranking.** It is a targeted follow-up on existing recorded sets, with
   replicates nested inside instances. No p-value is computed for it and none
   should be.
5. **The larger-instance work measures resolution only.** It does **not**
   extend the connectivity or fragmentation findings past `n ≤ 20`. Those
   remain exactly as published.
6. **Equalising cardinality is not equalising effort.** WalkSAT's and CDCL's
   collection budgets were never calibrated against each other — a confound the
   manuscript itself names ("CDCL's tested budget gave it systematically lower
   mean coverage than WalkSAT on the same instances"). The companion pilot
   `extensions/solver-budget-selection-pilot-2026-10-27/` addresses that
   directly.
