\begin{abstract}
This technical report consolidates two follow-up studies to
\emph{Algorithmic Resolution and Geometric Distinguishability in SAT Solution
Spaces}~\cite{dejesus2026sat}. Both concern the \emph{covering-radius
resolution} $\epshat(A,F)$ of a recorded sample $A$ of satisfying assignments:
how far the worst solution of $F$ sits from the nearest recorded one. First, we
record exactly how $\epshat$ behaves when a formula is re-encoded with auxiliary
variables. Measured on the original variables it is exactly invariant; measured
on all variables it can move, by at most the number of auxiliary variables, and
that bound is attained. The additive inequality is classical, appearing for
codes in Cohen, Karpovsky, Mattson and Schatz~\cite{cohen1985covering}; what we
add is the solution-relative form, with both projection hypotheses stated
explicitly, and a margin criterion telling which certified comparisons survive a
re-encoding. Applied to our own certified data, 43 of 116 comparisons carry no
guarantee beyond zero auxiliary variables. Second, we report a small paired
implementation study of two changes to the measurement procedure itself:
tightening a lower bound to a witness's actual distance, and generating distance
constraints lazily. The tightening is sound but recovers nothing on any of 257
reconstructed measurements. Lazy constraint generation is exact and needs only
3--43\,\% of the distance constraints. On the three units whose radius
sequences we verified identical it reduces total time including certification
from $46.0$\,s to $18.9$\,s; a fourth unit was exact under both methods in the
original run but sits on the measurement budget and did not reproduce, so it is
excluded from paired claims. On the five units that do not resolve, the lazy
method is worse. We claim no
general speedup, no asymptotic improvement, no solver ranking, and no
complexity-class consequence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope, and what is new}

This is a consolidation report, not a new theory. It is written to be read
alongside the antecedent paper~\cite{dejesus2026sat}, which introduced the
observable, the experimental programme, and the solver comparisons that
motivated everything here. Three things in it are new relative to that paper:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item an explicit statement of the two hypotheses under which $\epshat$ is
      preserved by re-encoding, and of the sharp additive bound when it is not
      (\S\ref{sec:invariance});
\item a margin criterion for certified \emph{comparisons} of two samples, and
      its measured distribution over our own certified data
      (\S\ref{sec:margins});
\item a paired implementation study of two changes to the measurement
      procedure (\S\ref{sec:stage1}--\S\ref{sec:stage2}).
\end{enumerate}

Two things in it \emph{qualify} earlier interpretations. The antecedent paper
reported a solver ordering on $\epshat$; a later budget-and-selection control
found that ordering to depend on the selection rule as much as on the solver,
and the cross-solver predictive claim of the earlier work has been retracted in
the repository's claim ledger. Nothing in this report reinstates either.

Throughout we keep four categories visibly distinct: \textbf{theorems} (proved
here or quoted with attribution), \textbf{finite validation} (exhaustive checks
over small instances), \textbf{measured results} (this study's runs), and
\textbf{open questions}. Passing a finite test does not prove a universal claim,
and we never present one as if it did.

\begin{remark}[A naming caution]
``Covering-radius resolution'' is a \emph{geometric observable}: a distance in
the Hamming cube. It has nothing to do with \emph{resolution} as a propositional
proof system. We use the longer phrase, or simply $\epshat$, wherever confusion
is possible, and we never abbreviate it to ``resolution'' alone.
\end{remark}

\section{The observable and the exact query}

Let $F$ be a CNF formula on $n$ variables with satisfying set
$\SF = \mathrm{sat}(F) \neq \emptyset$, and let $A \subseteq \SF$ be a nonempty
\emph{recorded sample}: the distinct solutions some procedure actually wrote
down. Write $\dH$ for Hamming distance.

\begin{definition}[Covering-radius resolution]
\[
  \varepsilon(A,\SF) \;=\; \max_{x \in \SF}\ \min_{a \in A}\ \dH(x,a).
\]
\end{definition}

This is the smallest $\rho$ for which balls of radius $\rho$ around the recorded
sample cover every solution. It measures \emph{how well $A$ covers $\SF$
geometrically}, which is a different question from \emph{what fraction of $\SF$
lies in $A$}: a sample can have tiny coverage and small $\varepsilon$, or large
coverage and large $\varepsilon$. Coverage and covering-radius resolution are
logically independent, as established in the antecedent
work~\cite{dejesus2026sat}. For a code $C \subseteq \{0,1\}^n$ and
$\SF = \{0,1\}^n$ this is exactly the covering radius of coding
theory~\cite{cohen1997covering}.

\paragraph{The exact threshold query.}
$\varepsilon$ is computed, not estimated, through a family of SAT queries:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:query}
  \Qr(x) \;\equiv\; F(x) \;\wedge\; \bigwedge_{a \in A} \dH(x,a) \ge r+1 .
\end{equation}
Each distance constraint is a cardinality constraint over all $n$ variables,
encoded with the sequential counter of Sinz~\cite{sinz2005towards} via
PySAT~\cite{ignatiev2018pysat}. The query family is monotone in $r$, and
\[
  \Qr \text{ satisfiable} \Rightarrow \varepsilon \ge r+1,
  \qquad
  \Qr \text{ unsatisfiable} \Rightarrow \varepsilon \le r .
\]

\begin{observation}[Boundaries, exhaustively checked]\label{obs:oracle}
Over all $3\,679$ pairs $(A,\SF)$ arising from $n=4$: the ladder reconstructs
$\varepsilon$ exactly and is monotone in $r$ in every case; $Q_0$ is
unsatisfiable exactly when $A = \SF$, i.e.\ exactly when $\varepsilon = 0$; and
$Q_n$ demands distance $\ge n+1$ in an $n$-cube, so it is unsatisfiable
unconditionally and yields the sound but vacuous $\varepsilon \le n$. This is a
finite check at one small $n$, not a proof for all $n$; the $r=0$ and $r=n$
statements are immediate from the definitions.
\end{observation}

\paragraph{What makes a bound certified.}
A \emph{lower} bound requires a satisfying assignment that is independently
re-checked against $F$ and against the \emph{entire} recorded sample $A$. An
\emph{upper} bound requires a refutation: Glucose~3~\cite{audemard2009predicting}
emits a clausal proof in DRUP form, which is checked by
\texttt{drat-trim}~\cite{wetzler2014dratrim} at a pinned commit. An
\texttt{UNKNOWN} result, or an UNSAT whose proof was not checked, tightens
\emph{no} certified bound and is reported as such. Every interval in this report
is bracketed by evidence of these two kinds.

\section{Representation invariance}\label{sec:invariance}

Let $G$ be a formula on $n+p$ variables and let
$\pi : \{0,1\}^{n+p} \to \{0,1\}^n$ drop the auxiliary block. Two hypotheses are
used throughout; \emph{both} are required and neither implies the other.

\begin{description}[nosep]
\item[(H1) the solution set projects:] $\pi(S_G) = \SF$.
\item[(H2) the recorded sample projects:] $\pi(A') = A$ for the lifted sample
      $A' \subseteq S_G$.
\end{description}

(H1) is projected logical equivalence, which is exactly what projected knowledge
compilation certifies: Bryant, Tan and Heule~\cite{bryant2025certifying} partition
variables into data and existentially quantified ones and certify, with
HOL4-validated soundness, that a compiled representation is logically equivalent
to the quantified input. Their \emph{Skolem assignments} \emph{construct} a lift
satisfying (H2) for a sample one chooses --- which covers every lift used below
--- but they do not certify (H2) for an \emph{arbitrary recorded} sample. When
$A'$ is whatever a solver happened to record, (H2) is an assumption about the
recording procedure.

\begin{remark}[(H2) is set equality, not injectivity]\label{rem:inj}
Failure of injectivity of $\pi$ on $A'$ does \emph{not} violate (H2). Take
$A' = \{(0,0),(0,1)\}$ and $A = \{0\}$: then $\pi(A') = \{0\} = A$, so (H2)
holds while $\pi$ collapses two members. (H2) fails exactly when the projected
set \emph{omits or adds} an assignment relative to $A$. Non-injectivity is a
separate matter: it changes distinct-sample counts and can change sampling
weights, which affects cardinality-matched comparisons but not $\varepsilon$.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Invariance on the original coordinates]\label{lem:inv}
Assume \textup{(H1)} and \textup{(H2)}. Then
\[
  \varepsilon_x(A',G) \;:=\;
  \max_{v \in S_G}\ \min_{w \in A'}\ \dH(\pi v, \pi w)
  \;=\; \varepsilon(A,\SF).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\dH(\pi v, \pi w)$ depends on $w$ only through $\pi w$, and $\pi$ maps $A'$
onto $A$ by (H2), so the inner minimum equals
$\min_{a \in A}\dH(\pi v, a)$. By (H1) the outer maximum ranges over exactly
$\SF$.
\end{proof}

This is a restatement in metric language of the standard projection fact, and we
claim no novelty for it. Its practical content is that a measurement taken on
the original variables is untouched by auxiliary ones.

\begin{theorem}[Auxiliary-coordinate bound]\label{thm:D}
Assume \textup{(H1)} and \textup{(H2)}, and measure on all $n+p$ coordinates.
Then
\[
  \varepsilon(A,\SF) \;\le\; \varepsilon_{\mathrm{full}}(A',G)
  \;\le\; \varepsilon(A,\SF) + p ,
\]
and both bounds are attained.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $\dH(v,w) = \dH(\pi v,\pi w) + \dH(v|_{\mathrm{aux}}, w|_{\mathrm{aux}})
\ge \dH(\pi v,\pi w)$, the lower bound follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:inv}. For the
upper bound fix $v \in S_G$, put $x = \pi v \in \SF$, choose $a^\ast$ attaining
$\min_a \dH(x,a) \le \varepsilon$, and take $w \in A'$ with $\pi w = a^\ast$,
available by (H2); then
$\min_{w'} \dH(v,w') \le \dH(x,a^\ast) + \dH(v|_{\mathrm{aux}},w|_{\mathrm{aux}})
\le \varepsilon + p$. For attainment take free padding,
$S_G = \SF \times \{0,1\}^p$: lifting every $a \in A$ to every auxiliary value
gives $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{full}} = \varepsilon$, and placing all lifts at one
auxiliary value gives $\varepsilon + p$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Attribution.}
The additive-$p$ inequality is \textbf{classical}. Cohen, Karpovsky, Mattson and
Schatz~\cite{cohen1985covering}, Corollary~1, state that puncturing a code on
$p$ coordinates reduces its covering radius by at most $p$ --- the same
phenomenon read in the opposite direction. There the maximum defining the
covering radius runs over the \emph{whole} Hamming space; Theorem~\ref{thm:D} is
the solution-relative form, where it runs over $\SF$ and the ambient set is
coupled to the extension by (H1), and its proof is the classical one.
Theorem~\ref{thm:D} specialises to their statement when $\SF = \{0,1\}^n$. We
emphasise that a limited literature search does not establish priority; we
searched the covering-code and projected-counting literatures and report what we
found, not an exhaustive survey.

\begin{observation}[Sharpness and controls, exhaustively checked]\label{obs:controls}
Over small instances with exact integer arithmetic: variable permutations
composed with coordinate complementations preserve $\varepsilon$,
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{full}}$ and $|A|$ exactly ($153\,600$ (isometry, instance)
pairs, no discrepancy); a uniquely determined duplicate variable $z = x_0$ adds
no solution and leaves $\varepsilon_x$ untouched on all $120$ tested pairs, yet
moves $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{full}}$ on $80$ of them, because the all-coordinate
metric silently gives one original coordinate weight $2$; free padding attains
both ends of Theorem~\ref{thm:D} ($360$ instances each); and uniform sampling of
$S_G$ projects to the multiplicity-weighted law, with exact total variation $0$
for constant multiplicity and $1/6$ for $\mathrm{mult}(x)=1+[x_0{=}1]$ at $n=3$.
\end{observation}

\begin{remark}[What invariance does not say]\label{rem:cost}
Lemma~\ref{lem:inv} says a re-encoding cannot change the \emph{value} of
$\epshat$ measured on the original variables. It does \textbf{not} say that a
re-encoding cannot reduce the \emph{cost} of computing that value. Preserving a
quantity and cheapening its computation are compatible, and
\S\ref{sec:stage2} measures a case where a changed representation is in fact
cheaper. This report establishes no computational improvement from
\emph{projection} specifically; that is a gap in our evidence, not a theorem.
\end{remark}

\section{Comparison margins}\label{sec:margins}

Experiments compare two recorded samples on the same formula. Suppose
$A_1, A_2 \subseteq \SF$ with a certified strict margin
$M = \varepsilon(A_1,\SF) - \varepsilon(A_2,\SF) \ge 1$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:D1}
Measuring on all coordinates, under any extension by $p$ auxiliary variables
with lifts of both samples satisfying \textup{(H1)} and \textup{(H2)}:
if $M \ge p+1$ then
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{full}}(A_1') > \varepsilon_{\mathrm{full}}(A_2')$ for
\emph{every} such extension and lift; if $M \le p$ then \emph{some} extension
and lift ties the comparison \textup{(}at $M = p$\textup{)} or strictly reverses
it \textup{(}at $M \le p-1$\textup{)}.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If $M \ge p+1$ then by Theorem~\ref{thm:D},
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{full}}(A_1') \ge \varepsilon(A_1)
 = \varepsilon(A_2) + M > \varepsilon(A_2) + p
 \ge \varepsilon_{\mathrm{full}}(A_2')$.
For the second part use free padding with $A_2$ lifted to one auxiliary value
and $A_1$ to all, giving $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{full}}(A_2') = \varepsilon(A_2)+p$
and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{full}}(A_1') = \varepsilon(A_2)+M$ exactly.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[What a certified \emph{interval} establishes]\label{cor:D2}
In practice $M$ is certified only as $M \in [\ell,u]$ with $\ell \ge 1$.
Combining Corollary~\ref{cor:D1} with the interval gives three regimes:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
auxiliary variables & what the interval establishes \\
\midrule
$p < \ell$          & $M \ge \ell > p$: every allowed extension preserves the ordering \\
$\ell \le p < u$    & neither half applies: the interval alone does not settle it \\
$p \ge u$           & $M \le u \le p$: some allowed extension ties or reverses it \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
An exact margin is the degenerate case $\ell = u$, where the middle regime is
empty and the interval settles every $p$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Two cautions]
First, the second regime is an \emph{existence} statement over the theorem's
unrestricted permitted lifts. It does not say that a given encoding reverses a
given comparison, and adding auxiliary variables does not automatically reverse
anything. Second, the witnesses realising it use free padding with lifts of
unequal cardinality. If one additionally \emph{requires} matched lifted
cardinalities or unique extensions, the sharpness claim is not established by
the argument above and would need a separate proof; we do not claim it.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Measured distribution.}
Applying Corollary~\ref{cor:D2} to the certified comparisons recorded in this
repository --- $92$ with an exact margin and $24$ certified only as an interval,
$116$ in total --- gives: $43$ carry no guarantee beyond $p = 0$, of which $28$
have an exact margin of $1$ (so some single-auxiliary-variable extension ties
them) and $15$ are certified only as $M \ge 1$ with $u$ as large as $13$ (so
nothing follows at $p=1$, but a void verdict does follow at $p \ge u$). These are
\emph{comparisons}, with budgets, selection schemes and replicates nested within
at most $25$ instances; they are not $116$ independent observations. The
practical recommendation is one sentence: \emph{measure Hamming distance on the
original variables, and report the margin beside the verdict.} The repository
already does the former.

\section{Encoding-dependent observables}\label{sec:erv}

The antecedent note on encoding-relative variance~\cite{dejesus2026erv}
considers a language $\mathrm{LINSAT}$ of satisfiable GF(2) linear systems and
two polynomial-time encodings, and shows that a constraint-count observable has
different gradient signs under them. Three scope points matter when citing it
alongside $\epshat$, and all three were checked directly:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{It varies an observable, not a decision.} $\mathrm{LINSAT} \in P$
      under both encodings. On $300$ random systems the decision agreed between
      Gaussian elimination and brute force $300/300$, while the observable
      differed on $295/300$. Changing what a statistic reports is not changing
      what the problem decides.
\item \textbf{The closure encoding is exponential off a log-rank family.}
      The closure is spanned by whole equations, so
      $|\mathrm{Closure}(A,b)| = 2^{\operatorname{rank}[A \mid b]}$, the
      \emph{augmented} rank. $2^{\operatorname{rank}A}$ is wrong in general:
      the inconsistent pair $x=0$, $x=1$ has $|\mathrm{Closure}|=4$ while
      $2^{\operatorname{rank}A}=2$. The two ranks coincide exactly on consistent
      systems (Rouch\'e--Capelli). Since
      $\operatorname{rank}[A\mid b] \le \operatorname{rank}A + 1$, the
      log-rank polynomiality conclusion is unaffected.
\item \textbf{GF(2) results do not transfer to CNF-SAT by renaming.}
      $|\mathrm{sat}(\mathrm{OR}_k)| = 2^k-1 \neq 2^{k-1} =
      |\mathrm{sat}(\mathrm{XOR}_k)|$ for every $k \ge 2$.
\end{enumerate}

We therefore cite~\cite{dejesus2026erv} only for the point that
\emph{constraint-count} observables are encoding-relative. We do \textbf{not}
treat it as an established barrier to resolving P versus NP, and we do not treat
it as having predicted any subsequent result. Note also that $\epshat$ lies
outside its mechanism: $\epshat$ is a function of the solution set, invariant by
Lemma~\ref{lem:inv}, and its encoding dependence (\S\ref{sec:invariance}) comes
from a different source --- which coordinates the metric is taken over.

\section{Study 1: witness tightening}\label{sec:stage1}

Both measurement loops compute a witness's actual distance to the recorded
sample and then update the lower bound only to the requested threshold $r+1$.
Since a witness $x \in \SF$ with $\min_{a \in A}\dH(x,a) = d$ proves
$\varepsilon \ge d$, the sound update is $\mathrm{lo} \leftarrow \max(\mathrm{lo}, d)$.

We replayed every stored witness against the recorded sample for its
measurement, recovering samples through each stage's own hash-verifying loader
(both the formula hash and the sample hash were required to match; nothing was
repaired). Table~\ref{tab:stage1} reports the result: of $594$ witnesses, $79$ are
\emph{loose}, with maximum gain $6$; no witness produced a smaller bound, so the
tightening is sound on every one. The hard $n=64$ units contribute $0$ loose
witnesses out of $37$.

Table~\ref{tab:stage1agg} reports the quantity that matters. \textbf{No reconstructed measurement
would have obtained a better certified lower bound.} The reason is the radius
ladder: a loose witness at radius $r$ proves $\varepsilon \ge r+1+g$, but the
search then issues a query at a higher radius that also succeeds, and the final
bound is set by the highest successful radius.

Replaying each recorded radius sequence under the tightened rule, $21$ of $557$
pilot SAT queries and $0$ of $37$ hard-unit SAT queries would have been
redundant. This is a \emph{retrospective} count and a \emph{lower bound}: after
the first skip the policy would choose different radii, whose answers the replay
cannot know. \textbf{It is not a measurement of online runtime saving, and we do
not report one.}

\paragraph{Verdict.} Adopt the tightening: it is a one-line change, sound on all
$594$ stored witnesses, and it cannot lose. Do not expect a speedup from it.

\begin{conjecture}
Every one of the $37$ hard-unit witnesses has gain exactly $0$. A plausible
mechanism is that the cardinality constraint is binding --- the solver satisfies
$\dH \ge r+1$ tightly and has no pressure to exceed it --- but we did not test
this, and state it as an open question rather than an explanation.
\end{conjecture}

\section{Study 2: lazy distance-constraint generation}\label{sec:stage2}

\paragraph{The method.} Query~\eqref{eq:query} carries one distance constraint
per recorded solution. Write $Q_r^B$ for the query carrying constraints only for
$B \subseteq A$. The lazy method starts from a small $B$, solves $Q_r^B$, checks
any candidate against the \emph{whole} of $A$, and on failure adds the nearest
violated $a \in A$ and re-solves. This is the classical constraint-generation
loop of Dantzig, Fulkerson and Johnson~\cite{dantzig1954tsp}, and the
counterexample-guided abstraction refinement pattern of Clarke et
al.~\cite{clarke2000cegar,clarke2002satcegar} in its SAT-based
form~\cite{clarke2002satcegar}.

\paragraph{Exactness, stated before measuring.}
$\mathrm{sat}(Q_r^A) \subseteq \mathrm{sat}(Q_r^B)$ for $B \subseteq A$, since
$Q_r^B$ is a conjunction over a sub-family. Hence a \emph{checked} refutation of
$Q_r^B$ certifies $Q_r^A$ unsatisfiable, and a candidate satisfying $F$ with
$\min_{a \in A}\dH(x,a) \ge r+1$ is a valid witness for $Q_r^A$ whatever $B$
produced it. Verification is always against the full $A$. Refinement adds the
argmin, which the candidate violates, so no candidate recurs.

\begin{remark}[What a lazy refutation establishes, exactly]\label{rem:subset}
When $Q_r^B$ is refuted and the proof checks, what has been established is:
\emph{the subset $B \subseteq A$ already suffices to exclude every satisfying
assignment of $F$ at distance $> r$ from all of $B$} --- equivalently, balls of
radius $r$ about $B$ already cover $\SF$. It does \textbf{not} establish that
$B$ is minimal with this property, that it suffices at any smaller radius, or
that $\varepsilon(B,\SF) = \varepsilon(A,\SF)$. Each would need separate
evidence, and we claim none of them. The identifiers of each $B$ and its
relation to the full $A$ are archived with the run.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Design.} Nine experimental units, each recovered with both hashes
verified; two methods, static (S) and lazy (L), interleaved per unit with S
first; identical radius policy, encoder, solver and checker; a $120$\,s
per-measurement budget (reduced from the antecedent stage's $240$\,s, and stated
in the protocol frozen before measurement), a $60$\,s per-invocation solver
limit and a $120$\,s checker cap; initial $|B| = 4$ chosen by a deterministic
greedy farthest-point pass; refinement capped at $60$ iterations per query.
Timing covers encoding, solving, proof checking and refinement overhead, and the
budget is enforced on the measurement as a whole. Ratios are computed from
unrounded seconds. All $18$ measurements are reported; none was excluded.

\paragraph{Results.} Tables~\ref{tab:perunit}--\ref{tab:integrity} give per-unit figures, paired totals and
certification integrity. The pattern is a dichotomy:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Four units reached an exact answer under both methods} in the
      original run, agreeing with each other and with the historical exact
      values. On these, total time including certification falls from
      $165.3$\,s to $99.5$\,s --- though only three of the four survive the
      pairing check below. The gain is
      \emph{mainly} certification-driven --- checker time $82.7$\,s versus
      $18.6$\,s --- though not only: solve time also falls slightly, and
      encoding time rises, since each refinement re-encodes.
\item \textbf{Five units did not resolve under either method.} On four of them
      the lazy method ends with a \emph{wider} certified interval, and on one it
      ties; summed final widths are $46$ for static against $60$ for lazy. The
      cause is visible in the iteration counts: the lazy loop spent $48$--$59$
      refinements inside a \emph{single} radius query on three units, completing
      one query where the static method completed three.
\end{itemize}

Table~\ref{tab:totals} also reports the all-units aggregate, ratio $\approx 1.13$. \textbf{We do
not offer this as a headline.} It mixes units where both methods finished with
units where both merely exhausted the budget having completed different amounts
of work; where neither finished, the meaningful comparison is interval width,
and there the static method leads.

\paragraph{Pairing, verified rather than assumed --- and one unit fails it.}
Equal query counts do not show that two methods asked the same questions, so we
instrumented a re-run of the four units exact under both methods, recording the
radius sequence and per-query outcome with the formula and sample hashes
re-verified. Timings are \emph{not} taken from that run; the reported seconds
remain those of the original. Table~\ref{tab:pairing} gives the result, and it is not uniform:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Three units are verified paired} --- identical radius sequences,
      identical per-query outcomes, and the re-run reproduces the stage's
      certified interval, with both ladders terminating on their own rather than
      on the budget. On these three, total time falls from $46.0$\,s to
      $18.9$\,s (ratio $2.43$), with checker time $23.2$\,s against $3.2$\,s.
\item \textbf{One unit, \texttt{n64\_a2.5\_k0\_W}, is not.} Its static ladder hit
      the measurement budget. In the original run it returned an exact answer
      $\varepsilon = 26$ at the budget boundary; in the re-run it stopped on the
      budget at $[25,28]$ without reaching one, and the two methods asked
      different radius sequences ($[24,28,26]$ against $[24,28,26,25]$). Its
      stage-2 ``exact'' outcome under the static method is therefore
      \textbf{not reproducible}, and the unit is excluded from every paired
      claim.
\end{itemize}

We report both groupings in Table~\ref{tab:totals} and let the reader see the difference. The
four-unit figure of $165.3$\,s against $99.5$\,s is what was measured in the
original run; the three-unit figure is what survives pairing verification. We do
\emph{not} promote the three-unit ratio as a better headline merely because it is
larger: it rests on three units, and the excluded unit is excluded for a stated
reason, not for its number.

\paragraph{Constraints actually needed.} The lazy method terminated using
$3$--$43\,\%$ of the recorded sample's distance constraints. \emph{Fewer
constraints do suffice.} The obstacle on hard units is the number of refinement
iterations, not the final constraint count.

\begin{remark}[A non-sequitur we had to retract]
Removing constraints makes a refutation a logically \emph{stronger} statement,
since $\mathrm{sat}(Q_r^A) \subseteq \mathrm{sat}(Q_r^B)$. That is a statement
about logical strength only: \textbf{it does not force proof length or runtime
to increase.} On one unit the lazy proofs were indeed larger ($524\,081$ versus
$245\,460$ lines at $r=31$, with a $3.3\times$ smaller CNF), and we briefly
inferred that this was the general direction. The study's own data refutes that:
on the four resolved units the subset proofs are smaller and checker time falls
by a factor exceeding four. That unit is a measured counter-case, not a law.
\end{remark}

\section{Limitations, and the remaining question}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item This is a \textbf{small exploratory implementation study}: nine units at
      $n = 64$, one formula family, one solver, one encoding, one machine, no
      repetitions. It supports no population-wide claim, no asymptotic
      statement, no solver ranking, and no complexity-class consequence.
\item The $120$\,s budget is half the antecedent stage's. Three of the four
      resolved units finished well inside it; the fourth,
      \texttt{n64\_a2.5\_k0\_W}, sat on the budget boundary and did not
      reproduce. The five unresolved comparisons are budget-sensitive, and the
      widths quoted for them are specific to this budget.
\item Study~1's query saving is retrospective, not an online runtime
      measurement.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:D}'s sharpness is established for unrestricted permitted
      lifts; under matched lifted cardinalities or unique extensions it is open.
\item Wall-clock time does not equalise algorithmic work across tools of
      different implementation maturity, and instances here were not
      independently selected.
\end{itemize}

\begin{conjecture}[The remaining question]
The two regimes are cleanly separated by one observable we already record: the
refinement count. A method that begins lazily and reverts to the full encoding
once refinements exceed a threshold would, on this data, keep the certification
saving on the units that resolve and avoid the lost queries on the units that do
not. Whether that holds beyond these nine units is untested, and we deliberately
did not run it.
\end{conjecture}
